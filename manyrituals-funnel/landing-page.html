Sleep architecture,
not sleep hacks.

Most sleep content treats the symptom — you can't fall asleep, so here's a supplement. Here's a meditation. Here's a lavender pillow spray. These are symptomatic interventions. They address the surface feeling without touching the structure underneath.

Sleep quality is determined by what researchers call sleep architecture — the physical, hormonal, and neurological conditions your body needs to move through sleep stages correctly. Temperature. Cortisol timing. REM pressure. Adenosine clearance. Most generic advice doesn't touch these things, because explaining them requires understanding why the body sleeps, not just how to feel sleepier.

Bedroom Temperature

Here's an excerpt from the first ritual in the guide.

Day 1 — Tonight's ritual

Lower your bedroom to 18–19°C before sleep.

Your core body temperature needs to drop by approximately 1–1.5°C to initiate and maintain sleep. This is not optional — it is a hardwired biological requirement. Matthew Walker's lab at UC Berkeley has documented this extensively: when subjects slept in rooms above 22°C, their slow-wave (deep) sleep was measurably reduced compared to nights at 18°C.

The mechanism is vasodilation — your hands and feet act as radiators, releasing heat to drop your core. A warm room prevents this process. A cool room accelerates it.

The protocol:

  • Set thermostat to 18°C (65°F) or as close as your system allows
  • Direct a fan across the bed, not at your face
  • Wear socks if your feet feel cold — warm feet speed core cooling
