# CHANGES — Claims edited vs. original brief

This file documents every claim that was cut, softened, or re-attributed versus the original guide brief, and why.

---

## Night 1 — Temperature

- **Removed:** All references to "Walker's lab" and Walker (Why We Sleep). Matthew Walker's popular book contains claims that have not held up to scientific scrutiny in some areas. The underlying temperature-sleep mechanism is well-supported by independent research; we cite Harding 2019 and Okamoto-Mizuno 2012 instead.

---

## Night 2 — Phone out of the bedroom

- **Removed:** Drake 2015 / "mere presence vigilance" claim attributed to Drake. This citation is unverifiable and the claim appears misattributed in the original brief.
- **Retained** a note that if "mere presence" is mentioned in any future copy, it must cite Ward et al. (2017) as a cognition finding (not a sleep finding). This is marked VERIFY in the codebase.

---

## Night 3 — Dinner timing

- **Removed:** Spiegel 2004 (Annals of Internal Medicine) from Night 3. That paper is about sleep loss → appetite hormones (reverse causal direction). It would be misleading to use it for dinner timing. The claim is supported only by Crispim 2011 (VERIFY).
- **Softened:** Cortisol/growth-hormone mechanism dropped. No clean primary source was found and verified; the mechanism is plausible but not cited here.

---

## Night 4 — Alcohol

- **Removed:** "Ebrahim 2018 PLOS ONE" — this citation could not be verified. The well-documented Ebrahim 2013 (Alcoholism: Clinical and Experimental Research) is used instead and is marked VERIFIED.

---

## Night 5 — Warm bath

- **Corrected statistic:** Original brief had an imprecise citation of the Haghayegh 2019 meta-analysis. Corrected to: "17 studies included, 13 with usable data; approximately 10-minute average reduction in sleep onset latency; 1–2 hours before bed."

---

## Night 6 — Breathing

- **Jerath 2015 re-framed:** The original copy implied Jerath 2015 demonstrated measured outcomes. It is a theoretical/hypothesis paper. All references to it now use "proposes" rather than "shows." No cortisol or HRV outcomes are attributed to it.

---

## Night 7 — Position & pillow

- **Removed:** "supine → non-restorative sleep in 1,322 HUNT participants" claim. Skarpsno 2017 does not support this interpretation. The paper is cited only for "side sleeping is the predominant adult position."
- **Flagged:** Gordon pillow/cervical-spine citation — exact paper title, journal, and year could not be confirmed. Dropped from the guide until verified.
- **Labelled:** Lee 2015 (glymphatic/posture) is clearly labelled as an emerging rodent study throughout; described as "emerging research" only.

---

## General

- No dosages, diagnoses, or cure-language anywhere in the guide.
- Medical disclaimer is prominent and placed before the guide content begins.
- Every VERIFY item is soft-cited in the guide text (e.g., "research suggests…" rather than "a 2016 study found conclusively…") until the primary sources are confirmed.
